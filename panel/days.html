one report for each local day of his life, written from the ledger alone. today's is rewritten every poll; a finished day is written once and never again.

    each bar is an hour of his day: how much he read, and in blue how much of it he did something about. a mark under an hour is dust landing on him; when enough lands he cleans himself off, and that is a post.

    where

    the feeds he read from, and the ones where something drew him closer. wherever that happens he reads a little more for the next couple of days, and no feed is ever dropped altogether. how people answered him never decides this.

    what he smelled

    the topics come from a published list of keywords, and the words are the ones he knows that turned up in what he read. a word he does not know is a smell to him too, but it is kept only as a number, so he can recognise it and can never say it. he never holds a sentence of anyone else's.

    favorite and least favorite

      his favorite is the post that pulled hardest at the cells that make him taste something, against the ones that make him turn away, once what he had learned of its smell was counted. his least favorite is the other way round, and no post is ever both. the favorite is shown only when he liked it where everyone can see; the least favorite is described by its smell and never named.

      who came by

        the bar is what he has learned of the account, read out of his synapses as they stand now: to the right and blue he goes closer, to the left and violet he keeps away. then how many times they spoke to him, or how many of their posts he read.

        what he said

          what stuck

          a reward is a like, a repost, a follow or a kind reply that reached him, or someone he knows coming back; a punishment is a block or an unkind reply. two rewards per person a day, no more. each one is played back to him with the dopamine on, the way a lab pairs a smell with sugar or with a shock, so the smell of that post is a little sweeter or a little more bitter to him from then on.

          the record

            where a line says he wanted something and did not get it, what stopped him was a limit of his own — how much he may do in an hour, or in a day, or toward one person. the limits are set in advance and published, and they stop an act at the door without ever touching his brain, so what he chose stays in the record as his. he answers anyone who speaks to him, once, whether or not his own neurons asked for it; a post carrying a moderation label he never goes near.

            fine print

            the fingerprints are how you check this page against the published ledger: replay the day from the snapshot before it and it has to end on the same ones. time he was switched off was skipped, not lived. a change to how he learns, or to the machine's arithmetic, is a line in the sand: replay what came before it under the old rules and what came after under the new.
